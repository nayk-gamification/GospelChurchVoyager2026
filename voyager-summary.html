VOYAGER 2026

Our Journey Towards Spiritual Maturity

The retreat was called Voyager, focusing on spiritual maturity as a journey that we all take together. God's goal is that we grow towards maturity and become more like Jesus (Ephesians 4:13).

Lesson 1

Lesson 1: Understanding the World and Ourselves

The first lesson taught us that spiritual maturity requires us to understand the world and ourselves. However, our view of the world depends on the perspective from which we see it.

The speaker drew lessons from Ecclesiastes, where the reality of death makes everything appear meaningless, or vanity (Ecclesiastes 1:2-3; 3:19-20). When our perspective is dominated by death, we may begin to think that everything we do is meaningless, and even that our own lives have little value.

However, when we put Jesus at the centre, our perspective changes. Jesus is the way, the truth and the life (John 14:6). Through the life that Jesus gives us (John 10:10), we see the world differently. We recognise that God created the world to be good (Genesis 1:31). Similarly, we understand ourselves as people whom God loves and saves through Jesus (John 3:16). This gives us a new vision of ourselves and the world.

Lesson 2

Lesson 2: Immature Faith and Mature Faith

The second session explored immature faith and mature faith through Jacob's two journeys.

On his first journey, Jacob was fleeing from his brother. His faith was transactional. He approached God almost like making a bargain: "If you protect me and provide for me, then you will be my God" (Genesis 28:20-22). Transactional faith expects God to do something for us in return for what we promise or do for him.

Yet God continued to love and care for Jacob, even in his immaturity. This shows God's grace towards him.

On Jacob's second journey, as he returned home, he was confronted by God. Jacob wrestled with God, who seemed to come against him (Genesis 32:22-30). Through this encounter, Jacob had to learn to submit to God and receive his blessing in a new way.

Eventually, Jacob, who had spent his life seeking blessings, became someone who blessed others (Genesis 47:7, 10; 48:15-16). His journey reminds us that God works through our struggles to bring us towards greater maturity.

Lesson 3

Lesson 3: Our Desires and Temptations

The third session focused on our desires. What do we love, and what do we want?

The speaker used the example of the seductive woman in Proverbs 7, who tempts a young man through his senses and desires (Proverbs 7:6-23). Temptation can become so powerful that resisting it seems almost impossible.

However, with God's help, we can overcome temptation. Jesus himself faced temptation and resisted it (Matthew 4:1-11; Hebrews 4:15).

We must first recognise our temptations and genuinely desire to overcome them. We must also ask God for help. Jesus taught us to pray, "Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one" (Matthew 6:13).

We also learned that others face similar temptations. We can encourage and support one another as we struggle to overcome them (1 Corinthians 10:13; Galatians 6:1-2).

Lesson 4

Lesson 4: When God Seems Hidden

The fourth session, Dr Prince's final session, focused on spiritual maturity when God does not seem obviously present in our lives.

The speaker used the example of Esther. God is never directly mentioned in the book, yet his presence and work are implied throughout the story. We see events coming together in ways that human eyes cannot fully understand.

Esther had to act courageously without knowing what would happen (Esther 4:13-16). Similarly, we may experience times when God seems absent or silent.

Even when we cannot see him, God is still at work behind the scenes. We may not understand what he is doing, but we can continue trusting him, being patient and making wise decisions.

God's word and the church family he has given us help us remain faithful, even when we cannot clearly see what God is doing (Hebrews 10:23-25).

Lesson 5

Lesson 5: What Would Jesus Do?

The fifth session, led by Reena, reviewed the four earlier lessons and summarised them through one practical question: "What Would Jesus Do?"

This simple question can help us respond appropriately to the situations we face. When we are angry, what would Jesus do? When we are afraid, what would Jesus do? When we face temptation or disappointment, how would Jesus respond?

As we ask this question, we learn to think and act differently. We begin to shape our lives according to Jesus' example and become more like him in this world (1 John 2:6; 1 Peter 2:21).

Lesson 6

Lesson 6: Thinking, Feeling and Behaviour

The sixth session focused on three areas of spiritual maturity: our thinking, our feelings and our behaviour. Another way of describing these is Head (knowledge), Heart (emotions, inner life and character), and Hands (our actions and what we do in the world).

We saw that when we invest in only one area, we do not become what God wants us to be.

If we grow only in knowledge, we become like a lollipop, with a big head but little growth in our heart or actions. We may know a great deal, but that knowledge does not necessarily change how we live.

Similarly, we may have knowledge and be actively involved in ministry, but still lack love for God and people. We then become like an hourglass, large at the top and bottom, but narrow in the middle. Paul reminds us that even great knowledge and acts of service amount to nothing without love (1 Corinthians 13:1-3).

God looks at our hearts (1 Samuel 16:7). He wants our thinking, feelings and behaviour to grow together. Our inner lives should be shaped by God's love and by our love for others.

So our prayer is simple: Lord, change my heart.

Lesson 7

Lesson 7: Committing Our Desires to God

The final session, led by Sucharati, called us to commit our desires to God and become intentional about what we will do after the retreat.

The song This Is My Desire expressed our desire to honour God and offer our whole lives to him.

We want God to shape our thinking, feelings, desires and behaviour. We want to follow Jesus in practical ways, making choices that please him and learning to live differently in the world (Romans 12:1-2).

Other Activities During the Retreat

The Voyager Games

The Voyager Games included several activities that taught us different lessons about maturity. Through physical challenges, competitions, teamwork and difficult choices, we learned about knowing our limits, achieving goals, handling emotions, resisting temptations and helping others.

Some of the app-based games were also connected to key Bible verses. These activities helped us experience and remember the lessons of spiritual maturity.

The Campfire: We Are Not Alone

The campfire activity reminded us that our mentors, senior youth and even younger youth are all on the journey towards maturity.

Through their stories, we learned that others have faced struggles, failures and difficult experiences. Their journeys have not always been easy. Yet God has continued to work in their lives.

We learned that we are growing together, and we can encourage and support one another (Galatians 6:2).

We are not alone.

Our Commitments: Continuing the Journey

We ended the retreat by making personal commitments about something we want to work towards.

Each of us identified one area where we want to grow, whether in our thinking, feelings or behaviour. We also considered one simple step we could take to improve in that area.

We identified mentors and senior youth who could help and encourage us during the coming year.

Spiritual maturity is a lifelong journey. We are still growing, and God continues to work in us (Philippians 1:6).

Voyager reminded us that we are all on this journey together. We are not alone.

We are not alone.
